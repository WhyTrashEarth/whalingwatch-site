## Clearer ways into the reference

The homepage now explains the reference directly, displays Angga’s original illustrations, links to individual country profiles, and shows dated developments. Law & governance is in the main navigation. The footer is shorter, with the page directory and all five language choices still available.

The project background identifies WhyTrashEarth, Alec Flores and Angga. The research, historical source catalogue, open questions and free access remain part of the site.

## Six dated profiles

New profiles cover [Iceland](/countries/iceland/), [Norway](/countries/norway/), [Japan](/countries/japan/), [Greenland](/countries/greenland/), the [United States](/countries/united-states/) and [St. Vincent & the Grenadines](/countries/st-vincent-and-the-grenadines/). Each identifies the framework, species, inspected sources and limits of the review.

The Norway and Japan figures are attributed to government publications, with quota years and source dates visible. Limits are not presented as actual catches. IWC numerical catch-limit tables are linked rather than reproduced. Missing catch totals are not treated as zeros.

## Correction: Alaska and the Makah Tribe

The country index previously described bowhead and gray-whale catch limits together as applying to eligible Alaska Native communities. That wording conflated separate frameworks.

Alaska Native bowhead hunting operates within an IWC and federal co-management framework. The Makah Tribe’s eastern North Pacific gray-whale authorisation process concerns Washington State and has separate permit requirements. Neither a waiver nor a permit application establishes that a hunt occurred. The [United States profile](/countries/united-states/) links the IWC and NOAA records supporting this distinction.

## Accurate language and date labels

The Icelandic, Japanese, Norwegian Bokmål and Brazilian Portuguese pages remain short introductions. Their search metadata no longer identifies them as translations of the full English homepage. No unreviewed full-page translations have been published.

Structured page and article information identifies the project and publication dates. Sitemap modification dates are recorded per page rather than reset for every build. These changes help describe the site; they do not guarantee search ranking or indexing.
